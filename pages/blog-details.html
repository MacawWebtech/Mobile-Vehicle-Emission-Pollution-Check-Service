Emission limits explained: what the numbers on your certificate mean

Farah Shaikh, Quality & Calibration8 Oct 20266 min read

Your emission certificate has a row of numbers on it. Most people never look at them. But they tell you a lot about how healthy your engine is — and whether you'll pass next time.

The short version

An emission test measures what comes out of your exhaust while the engine runs. Each reading is compared with a limit. If every reading is under its limit, you pass.

PUC stands for Pollution Under Control. It's the certificate that proves your vehicle's emissions are within the legal limits.

What each number means

CO — carbon monoxide

A gas made when fuel doesn't burn completely. Shown as a percentage. A high CO reading often means the engine is getting too much fuel or not enough air — a clogged air filter is a common cause.

HC — hydrocarbons (unburnt fuel)

Fuel that went through the engine without burning, shown in parts per million (ppm). High HC can point to worn spark plugs or a misfire.

Lambda — the air-to-fuel balance

For newer petrol cars, lambda shows whether the mix of air and fuel is balanced. A value close to 1.00 is ideal.

Smoke opacity — for diesel vehicles

Diesel engines are tested for soot. A light beam passes through the exhaust smoke, and the test measures how much light is blocked.

A well-maintained vehicle usually passes with plenty of room to spare. Small fixes make a big difference.

How to stay well under the limit

  • Service your vehicle on schedule — especially the air filter and spark plugs.
  • Use the fuel grade your manufacturer recommends.
  • Don't ignore a check-engine light.
  • Drive for 10 minutes before the test so the engine is warm.
